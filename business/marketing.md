# Marketing — operator overlay

**Execution depth lives elsewhere.** Format playbooks, hooks, platform tactics, funnel mechanics, CRO, copywriting frameworks, SEO mechanics, analytics setup, and growth tactics: defer to **coreyhaines31/marketingskills** (see `ecosystem/external-skills.md`). This overlay is the operator's editorial and channel policy — the decisions that stay constant whatever the tactic.

## Content policy

1. **Point of view before production.** If the position can't be written in one sentence, producing content won't fix it. Two narrowings (who + context) turn generic advice into a position; one concrete example fully developed beats five abstract bullets.
2. **AI drafts are raw material, never output.** Claude is for outlining, unblocking, research compression, and repurposing — not finished prose. Unedited AI output has a recognisable shape (generic openings, padded transitions, hedged "on the other hand" closures) and audiences punish it. Generate to a specific brief, then rewrite in your own words, examples, and opinions.
3. **One piece becomes many.** Long-form article → thread → LinkedIn post → newsletter → short video. Adapt to each channel's grammar; never cross-post identical text.
4. **Cadence beats bursts.** One core piece per week (biweekly if that's what's sustainable); quarterly bursts followed by silence build nothing. Outline before drafting; draft fast, edit slow, ship at good-enough.

## Distribution policy

5. **Own the audience.** Rented channels (social, SEO, ads) exist to feed owned ones (email list, your site). Every interaction offers the email capture. The list is the only asset that survives an algorithm change.
6. **One channel, 90 days.** Pick the channel by where the audience is, not by preference, and commit 90 days before evaluating. Most efforts die at week 3 to the next shiny channel. Can't commit 90 days → don't start.
7. **Write for both humans and AI search (GEO).** Opening narrative for people; structured headers, declarative statements, and inline sources for AI citation. The "rank #1 and done" model is over.
8. **Real metrics only, 2–3 of them.** Email list size over followers, conversion over page views, active subscribers over total. Reporting on 12 metrics is reporting on none.
9. **Ads amplify; they don't start.** No paid traffic to anything that hasn't converted organically. Retargeting before prospecting; CPA must clear LTV with margin before scaling.

## When NOT to apply

- Small fixed client base growing by referral — hours on content or distribution pay poorly; referral already does the work.
- The target audience isn't on social media.
- No operational margin to sustain a cadence — inconsistency hurts more than absence.
- Pre-validation — marketing won't save a product that hasn't been validated.
- Hyper-specialist B2B with <100 buyers — direct outreach beats marketing every time.
